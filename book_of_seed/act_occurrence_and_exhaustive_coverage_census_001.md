# Act occurrence and exhaustive coverage census 001

## Question

Does one exact Act occurrence need another prior occurrence to account for its
existence?

Or is the Act occurrence itself the exact constitutional fact, with exhaustive
coverage established separately only when an exact bounded member set is
completely acted upon?

This census changes no Book clause, runtime occurrence, Act, result, relation,
identity, or reader.

## Regress falsifier

Suppose one prior occurrence were mandatory merely because an Act occurred:

```text
prior occurrence
        ↓
Act occurrence
```

The same demand would apply to the prior occurrence:

```text
another prior occurrence
        ↓
prior occurrence
        ↓
Act occurrence
```

The active Book establishes Acts and their exact occurrence coordinates. It
does not establish a universal earlier occurrence whose additional physiology
accounts for each Act. Demanding one would create a regress rather than recover
an omitted distinction.

The narrower test is therefore:

> Do equally exact members exist for which the family records no Act, while
> another member receives the Act?

If yes, the missing discriminator is real. If every member of an exact bounded
set receives one Act, no selection distinction exists to explain.

## Disposition

Withdraw the generic prior-Act-occurrence vacancy.

Do not remove the active variable Applicability family merely because no prior
occurrence accounts for its Acts. Pressure its two pre-Applicability binding
events instead. The falsifier must preserve:


If those states survive without either binding event, binding remains exact
coordinates while Applicability remains the occurrence that answers the exact
