# Bounded, complete, and exhaustive census 001

## Question

Do `bounded`, `complete`, and `exhaustive` repeat one claim that nothing is
missing, or do they distinguish independently variable coordinate domains?

This census changes no active Book clause, admission, machine grammar,
runtime occurrence, Act, result, relation, identity, or reader.

## `bounded`: exact extent

An exact boundary determines which coordinates are available to one reading or
Act. It does not say that every coordinate inside that extent has been acted
on.

The Applicability interruption control is decisive:

```text
through B1
exact cross-set pairs    4
recorded Act subjects    1

bounded                  yes
exhaustive               no
```

Thus an exact extent can exist while work over its coordinates remains
non-exhaustive.

## `exhaustive`: no omitted coordinate

At a later selected boundary:

```text
through B2
exact cross-set pairs    9
recorded Act subjects    9

bounded                  yes
exhaustive               yes
```

`exhaustive` compares two exact addressed surfaces at one selected boundary.
It is false when one exact coordinate in the bounded subject extent has no Act
and true when none is omitted.

It does not create the boundary, freeze future work, or validate the internal
shape of a result.

## `complete`: required structural branches

The active `04.Compare.B` use concerns the findings inside one exact recorded
Compare result.

That result has four named finding branches:

```text
same-content findings
conflicting findings
findings of the earlier result
findings of the later result
```

An empty branch remains a required branch. The reader refuses a result whose
finding mapping omits, adds, renames, or changes the type of one branch. It
then addresses every exact entry by the owning Compare occurrence, finding
category, and finding position.

`complete` therefore concerns the exact structural closure of one result, not
coverage of an external subject extent.

## Independent controls

A complete Compare finding structure can be read without claiming that every
other co-current subject pair received an Act.

Conversely, exhaustive Applicability coverage at one boundary can exist before
any positive Compare Act or Compare result exists. It says nothing about the
structural branches of a later result.

```text
bounded + not exhaustive     exact interruption state
bounded + exhaustive         exact coverage state
complete result              exact result-shape state
complete result              no cross-set coverage claim
exhaustive coverage          no result-shape claim
```

## Completeness boundary

A completeness boundary supplies the exact extent under which findings are
claimed. It does not itself make a finding structure complete or a subject
surface exhaustive.

```text
completeness boundary    extent coordinate
complete findings        required result branches present
exhaustive subjects      no bounded subject coordinate omitted
```

The common root does not collapse these coordinate roles.

## Finding

```text
bounded       exact extent is addressed
complete      required structural coordinates of one result are present
exhaustive    no coordinate in an exact bounded extent is omitted
```

All three conditions can vary independently under the controls above.

## Disposition

Keep `bounded`, `complete`, `completeness`, and `exhaustive` in active Book
admission.

Their qualified domains are load-bearing. None establishes a Population,
work-progress surface, completion occurrence, or future claim.
