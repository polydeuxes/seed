## 1. Native grammar that may supply the question shape

### 1.1 Relation Assertion anatomy

Active `01.Standing.D` requires each exact relation to have its exact subjects
and responsible occurrence. `01.Standing.D.1` says an Assertion is exact
content as one subject and carries its source, provenance, Authority, Scope,
Locality, conflicts, limits, Unknown, and Standing coordinates.

Active `01.Standing.E` adds the relation-Assertion anatomy:

```text

exact relation content
second subject
```

Its Standing additionally requires the exact relation occurrence, Authority,
Scope, Locality, limits, conflicts, and Unknown
(`book_of_seed/chapters/01_constitutional_standing.md:17-37`).

The active machine witness preserves the same requirement:

```json

    "first_subject",

    "second_subject"

    "exact_relation_occurrence",
    "Authority",
    "Scope",
    "Locality",
    "limits",
    "conflicts",
    "Unknown"

```

This is constitutional demand concerning an already exact relation Assertion.
It is not an acquisition rule, a source segmentation rule, or a mapping from
source positions to those coordinates.

### 1.2 What the demand legitimately asks

The active requirement can legitimately expose these unresolved questions for
one proposed relation Assertion:

```text
what is the exact first subject?

```

It cannot answer any of them by identity. In particular:

```text

!= three source results assigned to those coordinates

!= relation-Assertion coordinate order
```

The grammar therefore supplies the shape of the question, but active law does
not make that shape an exact Compare rule against unknown material.
