# Stopping and Completion

## Constitutional subject
The bounded Stop and the stronger Assertion that a bounded result is complete.

## Core distinction
What exact Responsibility, Act, occurrence, Authority, Scope, and result establish a Stop or completion Assertion?

## Bounded resolution
A reason participates in a stopping Act only through an exact support relation under that Act's Responsibility, Authority, Scope, and limits.

Where a bounded Stop is established, the stopping occurrence preserves the exact supported reason and Stop result. No other Assertion, result, or occurrence supplies that Stop.

## Important distinctions
- stopping != completion
- stopping != failure
- no movement != no required result
- bounded exhaustion != global impossibility
- local stop != termination outside its Scope
- a preventing Assertion != a Stopping occurrence

## Counterexamples or failure modes
- Declaring completion because no candidate Act occurred.
- Continuing movement after the bounded result has satisfied Standing.
