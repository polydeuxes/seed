# Stopping and Completion

## Constitutional subject
The bounded Stop or absence of movement and the stronger Assertion that a bounded result is complete.

## Core distinction
Which exact evidenced material or Standing can support a bounded responsible exact Act stopping its present act, a responsible stopping occurrence at a competent boundary that establishes a bounded stop, or the stronger completion Assertion for an exact bounded result?

## Bounded resolution
Evidence supports a reason for non-movement only through an exact support relation. Candidate reasons include an Assertion, exhaustion or impossibility finding, operator boundary, resource limit, preservation failure, or satisfied Scope. No emitted representation, status field, or Assertion supplies Stopping or completion by identity.

Where a bounded Stop is established, the stopping occurrence preserves the exact supported reason. Absence of a recorded stopping occurrence or reason Evidence establishes no Stop. Material evidenced as inapplicable, unsupported material, missing required binding, outside-Authority Standing, and an Unknown established for a required binding each require exact supporting coordinates. This discipline cannot assign Stopping the reason-establishing Responsibility.

## Important distinctions
- stopping != completion
- stopping != failure
- no movement != no required result
- bounded exhaustion != global impossibility
- local stop != termination outside its Scope
- source-labelled wording != Stopping by identity
- a preventing Assertion != a Stopping occurrence

## Counterexamples or failure modes
- Declaring completion because no candidate Act occurred.
- Continuing movement after the bounded result has satisfied Standing.

## Constrained movement stopping correction 001

An exact Act responds to an Assertion, Unknown, resource limit, preservation failure, satisfied Scope, or operator boundary only through its assigned Responsibility and exact occurrence. Its result carries a Stop, deferral, narrowing, a Representation carrying material and an unmet requirement Assertion, or a refusal to establish a support relation only where established by that occurrence. None makes the asserted failure or completion by identity. Unknown supports no movement, and an Assertion result establishes no later stopping occurrence.

Stopping is not failure.
