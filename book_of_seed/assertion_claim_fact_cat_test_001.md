# Assertion, Claim and Fact: cat test 001

Findings only. No Book or runtime amendment.

## The three outcomes differ

The operator asked whether `Claim` and `Fact` are cats now that `Assertion` has
`01.Standing.D.1`. Three applications, three different results — which is what
the test does when it is working.

```text
  Assertion   PASSES.  Owner named, producing Act named, and the clause
                       explicitly refuses to relocate production into the result.
  Claim       CARRIED, not owned.  No producing Act names it, no clause gives it
                       a Responsibility, and the Book declines to make it a
                       durable subject at all.
  Fact        Fails a different test.  It has a producing boundary. It has two
                       meanings, and its own clause says they must not collapse.
```

## 1. Assertion

**Does it name something?** Yes. `01.Standing.D.1`: "the exact asserted content
as its own subject under its carried Evidence, provenance, Scope, Authority,
conflicts, limits, **Unknowns**, and current Standing."

**Does it own a Responsibility, or is it carried?** **Owned, and named:** "An
Assertion owns the Responsibility for fidelity of its Standing to those
applicable coordinates," bounded immediately — "local to that bounded Assertion;
it does not create a universal assertion manager, knowledge owner, or conflict
resolver."

**What Act produces it, and what Standing does that establish?** "A responsible
Act may produce an Assertion and establish only the bounded Standing warranted
by that Act's Evidence and Authority."

**[measured]** The clause is not self-instantiating, and it says so in the
sentence a reader would reach for to attack it: "The Assertion does not thereby
perform or own the Act that produced it, produce itself, or become its Producer
or producing occurrence... Producer != Assertion owner, producing Act !=
Assertion Responsibility, and recorded occurrence != Producer."

**[inference]** That is `#2439`'s correction absorbed into active law. The
Assertion owns *fidelity of an already-established Standing*, not the
establishment of it, so the owner and the warrant are different subjects. The
circular-grammar failure `#2168` was withdrawn for is not present here.

## 2. Claim — carried, and the Book says so

**[measured]** `05.Testimony:10` defines a Claim as "the semantic proposition
**carried by** testimony, observations, evidence payloads, fact artifacts,
relationships, projections, explanations, documentation, or consumer
assertions."

**[measured]** No clause in active law names an Act producing a Claim as a
Claim. No clause gives a Claim a Responsibility — a search for a Claim owning or
being responsible for anything returns nothing.

**[measured]** The Book positively declines the subject: "The Book does not
currently recognize a separate durable `Claim` artifact as the universal subject
of knowledge."

**[inference]** This is the shape `confidence` had in `#2418`: a real word,
appearing 94 times, carried by eight other things, with no producing act and no
owner. `claim-centricity` is doing work — the clause's point is that the
proposition **is not identical to any one storage object** — but that is a
constraint on other subjects, not a subject.

**[inference]** So Claim is a cat, and **the comparison to `confidence` is
wrong** — this report's author drew it and the operator corrected it.

```text
  confidence   named home, named owner: the provider, outside Seed
               01.External:15 admits it as attributed source grammar
  claim        carried by eight things, no owner anywhere, no producing act
```

`confidence` survived `#2418` because something owns it and that something is
external. `claim` has no owner in or out of Seed. Under `#2361`'s rule — the
Book does not carry ordinary words — that is not a coordinate to leave alone.
**`claim` must eventually be removed.**

**[measured]** The scale: 101 occurrences across 17 of the Book's 31 active-law
files. Comparable to `artifact`'s 107, and larger than most banned families.
`claim-centricity` at `05.Testimony:10` is the load-bearing use, and it is
already a statement that the proposition is not identical to any storage object
— which is a constraint on other subjects, expressible without the noun.

## 3. Fact — a different failure

**[measured]** Fact has a producing boundary. `05.Testimony:16`: "A Fact
emission warrants that bounded standing for the proposition itself."

**[measured]** What it has instead is two meanings, and `05.Testimony:14` opens
by warning about exactly that: "A Fact has two safe meanings **that must not be
collapsed**" — a `Fact` artifact with fields, and a Fact as "an established,
evidence-backed, normalized, scoped claim."

**[inference]** Fact does not fail for lack of an owner. It carries a hazard its
own clause names and then relies on the reader to respect. That is a different
defect from Claim's and should not be repaired the same way.

## 4. The finding the operator did not ask for

**[measured]** `01.External.D:25` already owns the word the new clause turns on:

> A **Fidelity finding** is produced only by a bounded comparison that brings
> constitutional grammar, a bounded expectation, and an implementation witness
> under a declared seam or scope.

**[measured]** `01.Standing.D.1` gives an Assertion "the Responsibility for
**fidelity** of its Standing to those applicable coordinates."

```text
  01.External.D     subject   constitutional grammar vs implementation witness
                    producer  a bounded comparison, exclusively
                    result    a Fidelity finding

  01.Standing.D.1   subject   an Assertion's Standing vs its carried coordinates
                    owner     the Assertion
```

**[inference]** Two subjects, two owners, one word. If `01.Standing.D.1`'s
fidelity is `01.External.D`'s Fidelity, an Assertion owns something that clause
says is *produced only by* a bounded comparison. If it is not, then active law
now uses `fidelity` for two things — which is precisely the hazard
`05.Testimony:14` names for Fact, newly introduced rather than inherited.

**[Unknown]** Which reading is intended. This report does not choose, and the
distinction matters more than the word: `01.External:40` already holds that a
"Fidelity-shaped artifact != lawfully produced Fidelity finding."

## 5. What enforces an owned fidelity

**[Unknown]** An Assertion is preserved content. What occurrence fails when its
Standing stops corresponding to its coordinates, and what does that failure
produce? `01.External.D` answers this for Fidelity findings — a comparison
produces one — but `01.Standing.D.1` names an owner without naming what the
owner does or what happens when it cannot.

**[inference]** That is not fatal and it is not the circular-grammar defect. A
Responsibility whose discharge is unnamed is weaker than one whose discharge is
named, and recording that it is unnamed is cheaper now than discovering it when
something depends on the fidelity being enforced.

## What this does not establish

**How `Claim` should be excised.** That it must be is the operator's standing
rule under `#2361`; what replaces `claim-centricity`'s work at `05.Testimony:10`
is not recovered here, and the clause is doing something real. Naming the
requirement is not proposing the repair.

**That `Fact`'s two meanings should be split.** Its clause has held them apart
deliberately since before this session. Naming the hazard is not proposing the
repair.

**That `01.Standing.D.1` is defective.** It passes the test it was written for.
§4 and §5 record two questions it raises, neither of which is answered here.

**That three cases exhaust the family.** `Evidence`, `Standing`, `Testimony` and
`Finding` were not tested, and `#2409` holds that exhausting a finite list is
not Stopping.
