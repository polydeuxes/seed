# predicate: cat test 001

## 1. Executive

The question was whether `predicate` is just a measurement.

**Active law neither establishes nor refutes it.** `predicate` appears in two
senses that no clause relates. The question cannot be answered from the corpus
as it stands.

**The runtime cannot answer it either, and the attempt is where the real
finding is.** `predicate_catalog/core.json` asserts a
`measurement`/`durable_fact` distinction across 76 rows carrying **no source,
no evidence, and no provenance field**. Reading that catalogue as a refutation
uses the artifact of the assumption to prove the assumption. `01.Kinds:19`
disposes of it directly.

**What the catalogue is, is a specimen.** 76 rows defining "one canonical thing
Seed can know", authored whole, with no producing occurrence and nothing
recoverable behind any row. That is a compiled competency, and it is the same
shape as the EvidenceGraph gap.

**The cat test still returns something.** Step 4 finds a named producing act —
normalization — whose own clause denies that it establishes anything.

## 2. What the corpus establishes

```text
                sense A                      sense B
clauses         05.Testimony:12, :14,        01.External:28
                :16, :18; 06.Events:51
reads           "Seed's subject,             "the result of a declared
                predicate, value …           predicate"
                vocabulary"
names           what is asserted about       a bounded test whose result
                a subject                    a measurement may report
occurrences     five clauses                 one clause
```

No clause relates the two. Neither identity nor distinctness is recovered.

Sense B is the reading that invites "predicate is just a measurement", and it
does not carry the identity even on its own terms: a measurement *reports the
result of* a declared predicate, which makes the predicate an input rather than
the measurement.

That is the whole of what active law supports. It is a statement about the
corpus, not about predicates.

## 3. The cardboard, named

The first version of this report argued the refutation from three runtime
observations. All three are void.

```text
claimed as evidence                        actual standing
76 catalogue entries, 70 durable_fact      an authored list
  to 6 measurement
PredicateDefinition.kind =                 a dataclass field
  Literal["measurement", "durable_fact"]
901 uses across 49 modules                 occurrence frequency
```

`01.Kinds:19` names these forms and denies them:

> A kind label, artifact form, characterization category, **dataclass name**,
> concordance entry, **inventory row**, or recurring report type may preserve a
> bounded standing distinction for the subject and consumer it names. It does
> not close Seed's constitutional ontology, make all similarly named materials
> equivalent, establish a mandatory artifact grammar, or **supply kind-specific
> production authority without preserved evidence, provenance, scope,
> confidence limits, Unknowns, and the applicable production or establishment
> boundary**.

Every entry in `predicate_catalog/core.json` carries exactly five fields:

```text
predicate  kind  value_type  cardinality  allowed_values
```

No source. No evidence. No provenance. No scope. No Unknowns. No production
boundary. The clause's entire list of what would be required is absent.

So the catalogue's `kind` field is testimony that its authors distinguished
measurement from durable fact. It is evidence about the authors. It is not
evidence about predicates, and counting its rows measures only how many rows
were written.

The count argument was also circular in form. "If the terms named one thing,
seventy entries would be misclassified" assumes the classification tracks
something. That is the proposition under test.

## 4. What the catalogue actually surfaces

Demoting it as evidence promotes it as a subject.

`PredicateDefinition` is documented as "Definition of one canonical thing Seed
can know." 76 such things are asserted in one authored file. Nothing behind any
row is recoverable: no occurrence produced it, no source is named, no evidence
supports it, and no boundary established it.

That is a compiled competency — knowledge present in the runtime as a finished
list rather than as anything Seed came to hold. It sits alongside the
EvidenceGraph specimen rather than being a separate kind of problem.

The distinction the catalogue encodes may well be sound. The finding is that
its soundness is not recoverable from anything, which is why it cannot settle a
question about what `predicate` means.

## 5. The cat test

```text
1  does `predicate` name something?
     yes, in both senses — a normalization slot, and a declared test

2  what kind of thing?
     sense A: a coordinate within a stated vocabulary
     sense B: an input to a measurement

3  does it own a Responsibility, or is it carried by another?
     carried — normalization canonicalizes claims into it

4  what exact Act produces predicate-as-predicate, and what Standing
   does that production establish?
     normalization, and the same clause denies that it establishes
     anything
```

`05.Testimony:12`:

> Normalization canonicalizes an interpreted claim into Seed's subject,
> predicate, value, dimensions, time, source, and provenance vocabulary so it
> can be compared, supported, projected, contradicted, or explained.
> **Normalization does not create a new constitutional subject by itself, does
> not prove the interpreted claim, does not supply support**, and does not
> require a `NormalizedClaim` class or schema.

Against the prior cases:

```text
artifact    no producing act        no standing follows
record      producing act named     stated standing follows
pattern     no producing act        absorbed by existing findings
predicate   producing act named     act's clause denies standing
```

Named act, denied standing. That is what makes `predicate` safe to write down
and unsafe to lean on.

## 6. An unsupported possessive

`05.Testimony:12` writes "**Seed's** subject, predicate, value … vocabulary".

The same division appears in a source Seed holds —
`corpus/grammar_goold_brown.txt:38924`:

> …the agent, subject, or nominative, is that of which something is said,
> affirmed, or denied; that the attribute, verb, or **predicate, is that which
> is said, affirmed, or denied, of the subject**…

**This does not establish borrowing.** Two texts using one division is
resemblance, and `05.Evidence:30` lists similarity and co-occurrence among the
things that do not establish derivation. The first version of this report
claimed the vocabulary was borrowed. It does not.

What is established is narrower and still worth having: the possessive claims
nativeness, and no clause in active law records where the division came from.
The claim is unsupported, independently of where the division originated.

The practical bite is the same either way. A division presented as native
invites arguments from English intuition about what a predicate "really is",
and no clause licenses those.

## 7. Correction to `recurrence_consumer_first_recovery_001`

That report treated `the result of a declared predicate` as "an open slot for
any declared predicate". Given §2's sense split, the openness of that slot is
less certain than stated: sense B has one occurrence and no clause relating it
to sense A.

The load-bearing parts are unaffected. `01.External:28` still enumerates six
findings; `prefix occurrence` still covers the structural-shape case without
`pattern`.

## 8. What this does not establish

**That `predicate` is or is not a measurement.** §2 records that active law
does not say, and §3 records that the runtime cannot be asked. The original
question is returned unanswered.

**That the catalogue is wrong.** §4 states the opposite risk: it may be
entirely sound and still unrecoverable.

**That the two senses are two things.** No clause relates them, and absence of
a relating clause is not a distinctness finding.

**That the division was borrowed from the grammar corpus.** §6 withdraws that
claim. Only the missing provenance survives.

**That `predicate` should be retired or kept.** Nothing here argues either.

**Anything about what the grammar corpus asserts.** §6 quotes one passage to
establish that a division appears there. What that source asserts, and what
standing Seed may establish about it, is a separate responsibility and belongs
in its own document.
