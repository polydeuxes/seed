# Proposed-input constitutional shape recovery 001

## Scope and method

This report answers one question:

```text
What makes a preserved constitutional subject
 a proposed input to an exact Act?
```

It reads `proposed` against every active-law use of **proposed input**, and
separately checks the active uses of proposal, candidate and candidate
formation, Demand, input, participant, and Applicability. It treats the
applicability side recovered by #2333/#2334 as a boundary condition, not as a
question to reopen.

This is exactly one report. It amends no active law, runtime, test, projection,
or prior report, and constructs nothing. Verified at `f83d332`.

## Executive finding

**`Proposed input` is not recovered as an independent constitutional result,
Standing, kind, relation Standing, or produced artifact.** In active law it is
the local antecedent role of an exact subject being put forward for possible
participation, consumption, or reliance in one exact Act. The role is indexed
to that Act and proposed use; it is not a generic status the subject carries
from Act to Act.

The Book does not recover a distinct Proposal Act or universal Proposal
Responsibility. Nor does it identify the exact occurrence or event by which the
act-local role is assigned. The only positive ownership recovered at this seam
is narrower: the exact Act-owning Responsibility owns ensuring applicability
is determined for subjects already in that local proposed-input role. It may do
that in the same bounded occurrence that performs the Act. Active law does not
say that this applicability ownership, by itself, produced the antecedent
proposal.

Thus `proposed` most strongly names **a local role within an exact Act's input
grammar**, with its assignment occurrence unresolved. It is not an additional
constitutional noun merely because the clause uses an adjective.

```text
preserved subject
       │
       │ exact subject is put forward in the input role for one exact Act
       │ role is local; assignment act/occurrence and positive producer Unknown
       ▼
proposed input to exact Act
       │
       │ exact Act owner must ensure Applicability is determined
       ▼
Applicability determination
```

The first edge is recovered only in shape, not in producer occurrence.

## 1. Is `proposed input` a constitutional result or Standing?

**No independent result or Standing is established by active law.**

`01.Standing.E.1` repeatedly uses *proposed input* as the antecedent to an
Applicability determination. It expressly names the possible results of that
determination—**applicable**, **inapplicable**, **conflicting**, or
**Unknown**—and says that the exact applicability result remains recoverable.
It never names `proposed`, `proposal`, or `proposed-input standing` as a result.

This contrast is decisive:

```text
proposed input                 antecedent role named in the rule
Applicability determination   independently recoverable claim
applicable / inapplicable /
conflicting / Unknown          named possible results
```

The Book knows how to require independent Standing when that is intended. It
requires relation claims to preserve their dimensions; it distinguishes
candidate relation, relation testimony, and established relation Standing; and
it requires candidate production dimensions. None of those production or
Standing rules is attached to `proposed input`.

`02.Act` supplies only `proposal != occurrence`. That denial prevents a
proposal-shaped artifact or expression from proving an Act occurrence. It does
not positively establish Proposal as an Act, kind, Standing, or artifact.
Similarly, `01.External.A/G` permits material or translation to carry or form a
*claim proposal*, *relation proposal*, or proposed meaning assertion. Those are
attributed assertion forms, expressly short of warrant. They do not establish
a generic input-proposal Standing.

**Disposition:** local role, not independently recovered constitutional
Standing. Whether some future exact family could separately establish a
proposal claim is not answered; no such general result exists in current law.

## 2. What Act or occurrence produces proposal?

**Unknown. No distinct Proposal Act or producing occurrence is assigned.**

The strongest positive statement is `01.External.A`: translated material may
later be proposed *to another exact responsibility only through that
responsibility's independently established boundaries*. This fixes the target
locality and denies translation-by-identity as proposal. It does not name the
boundary, producer, occurrence, result, or evidence that performs proposal.

`01.Standing.E.1` is equally careful. It assigns the act owner responsibility
for ensuring Applicability, allows that determination in the same bounded
occurrence, and says that one occurrence may determine Applicability, exclude
inputs, perform or not perform the Act, and establish output Standing where
independently warranted. Proposal is absent from the list of independently
recoverable claims. Same occurrence therefore is permitted for the downstream
determination and Act; it does not prove an unmentioned proposal-producing
Act.

The Book also blocks reverse construction. Conditional input applicability
does not establish a proposed input, producer, responsible occurrence,
candidate-formation demand, or Demand. A proposal occurrence cannot be
recovered by reading backward from Applicability.

```text
Proposal Act                         not recovered
proposal-producing occurrence        Unknown
proposal artifact/result             not recovered
proposal inferred from Applicability denied
```

## 3. What Responsibility owns proposal?

**No independently assigned proposal owner is recovered.**

The exact Act-owning Responsibility owns *ensuring Applicability is
determined*. That explicit duty begins after the grammar has identified a
proposed input. The Book does not say that owning this duty makes the owner the
producer of proposal, and responsibility for a downstream boundary must not be
silently extended upstream.

What can be recovered is the proposal role's locality: material is proposed to
an exact Responsibility; applicability is local to the exact input and target
Act; coordinates are local to the exact Act and proposed use; a later Consumer
owns its own Applicability. Therefore the role belongs **within the boundary of
the exact Act-owning Responsibility**, rather than to a universal proposal
service. That is boundary locality, not proof of a separate ownership claim.

```text
exact Act owner owns Applicability assurance     warranted
proposal is local to exact Act/Responsibility    warranted as role locality
exact Act owner produced proposal                Unknown
upstream producer owns downstream proposal       not established
caller/operator owns proposal                    not established
universal Proposal Responsibility                not recovered
```

Consumer Demand does not repair the missing assignment. Demand has its own
responsible establishment occurrence and states a required bounded condition;
it neither produces a candidate nor selects a mechanism. Nothing says Demand
assigns an exact subject the proposed-input role.

## 4. Generic across Acts or local to the exact Act?

**The grammar is generic; each proposal role is exact-Act-local.**

`01.Standing.E.1` states a generic default applicable to constitutional Acts,
but its object is always the exact input-to-act relation. It denies generic
usefulness, compatibility, availability, or possible relevance as sufficient;
says Applicability for one Act is not Applicability for another; and makes
required coordinates local to the exact Act and proposed use.

This supports a generic rule form:

```text
for any exact Act A:
    subject S may occupy the local role “proposed input to A”
    A's owner must ensure Applicability(S, A) is determined
```

It does **not** support a portable `Proposed(S)` status or generic proposal
relation that survives across Acts:

```text
proposed to Act A != proposed to Act B
proposed for use U1 != proposed for use U2 automatically
```

`05.Testimony.E` does not make the grammar Compare-specific. It positively
names independently preserved testimonies or findings that a bounded
comparison may consume and imposes preservation conditions during consumption.
It neither establishes how either input is proposed nor closes the kinds that
may ever be inputs. Compare is one local use of the generic E.1 grammar, not
the source of a universal Proposal noun.

## 5. Coordinates carried by proposal

**No universal proposal-coordinate payload is recovered.**

E.1 names coordinates for the Applicability determination: exact subject and
content, purpose, scope/locality, authority, participants/roles, Consumer
context, preserved limits, and—where applicable—Consumer identity, source and
provenance, Standing and Warrant, currentness and occurrence identity, known
loss, conflicts, Unknowns, and negative authority. It then expressly says no
coordinate is universally required merely because a subject is proposed as an
input.

Accordingly:

| Coordinate question | Recovery |
| --- | --- |
| exact subject identity | Necessary to the local reference: E.1 says the determination is local to the proposed input's exact identity. |
| exact target Act / responsible boundary | Necessary to distinguish the local role; there is no generic proposal status. |
| proposed use / purpose | E.1 makes the applicability coordinates local to exact Act and proposed use; purpose is required for the determination. |
| Consumer | A local Consumer context/identity may be preserved by Applicability; proposal has no separately specified Consumer field. |
| Scope / Authority / Evidence | Required as the exact Applicability determination or proposed use requires; not universally carried as Proposal Standing. |
| producer / occurrence / provenance | Not assigned for proposal; the preserved subject and candidate retain their own independently established coordinates. |

The minimum `subject + exact target Act/use` above identifies the role; it is
not a claim that active law mandates a Proposal artifact schema. All stronger
coordinate carriage belongs to the subject's existing Standing or to the
Applicability claim, not to an invented intermediate noun.

## 6. Can proposal and Applicability occur in one bounded occurrence?

**Not constitutionally prohibited, but not positively established as two
occurrences.**

E.1 explicitly permits the Act owner to determine Applicability within the
same bounded occurrence and permits one occurrence to determine, exclude,
perform or establish no Act occurrence, and possibly establish output
Standing. Because proposal is not recovered as a distinct Act or claim, the
Book supplies no required occurrence boundary that must precede Applicability.
An exact Act-owning occurrence can therefore begin with a subject in its local
proposed-input role and determine Applicability without a separately evidenced
Proposal occurrence.

But the Book does not positively say that this occurrence *produces proposal*.
The careful result is:

```text
separate Proposal occurrence required                 no
same occurrence may contain later Applicability       yes
same occurrence thereby proven to produce proposal    no / Unknown
```

Same bounded occurrence does not collapse claims. Applicability remains an
independently recoverable determination; participation and Act occurrence
remain separate even when performed together.

## 7. Hard distinctions

### Preserved subject, candidate, and candidate formation

A preserved subject exists with whatever Standing its responsible preservation
or production boundary established. Reachability creates only a possibility
for later action. Neither preservation, reachability, availability, nor
Standing assigns an exact-Act input role.

A candidate has its own production grammar: producer, source-role,
formation-occurrence, scope, authority, and provenance are preserved where
known. An Unknown formation occurrence does not mean no candidate exists, and
carriage or consumption does not relocate candidate production. This makes
candidate existence independently intelligible, but never says candidate
Standing proposes the candidate to an Act.

Candidate formation and proposal therefore cannot be collapsed:

```text
subject exists          != subject is proposed
candidate exists        != candidate is proposed
candidate production    != proposal to an exact Act
candidate carriage      != proposal
```

### Demand

Demand states a required result or condition under an exact Responsibility and
scope. Demand establishment does not produce a candidate, and capability
Demand content may constrain later candidate formation without producing one.
No active clause makes Demand a proposal producer.

```text
Demand != candidate formation != proposed-input role
Consumer Demand != ownership of proposal
```

### Selection

Selection consumes a bounded candidate set and makes a choice or lawful
non-selection. Candidate identity, eligibility, ranking, and uniqueness are
not selection. E.1 separately says Applicability does not itself establish
selection and a candidate result is not automatically selected or admitted.
Nothing makes selection the act that retroactively proposed its inputs.

```text
proposal != selection
candidate proposed (if established) != candidate selected
```

### Admission

Applicability is not Admission. Admission is Consumer-local only where the
exact Consumer act requires it, and Applicable material does not thereby become
admitted. Because proposal precedes Applicability in E.1's rule, it cannot be
silently identified with the still-later Admission boundary.

### Applicability

Applicability is the bounded determination of whether the exact input may
participate in the exact Act. It does not create the proposed input whose exact
input-to-act relation it evaluates; active law expressly denies that
conditional Applicability establishes a proposed input.

```text
proposal != Applicability
Applicable != proposal produced
```

### Participation

Participation, consumption, or reliance occurs only in the responsible Act.
Applicable means the input may participate; it does not establish that the Act
or participation occurred. Presented-set participation is also a separately
established local relation: the presented alternative, not its source
candidate by identity, participates in the set, and that participation does
not establish Applicability or later Act occurrence.

```text
proposed != participating
Applicable != participation
presented alternative participates != source candidate participates
```

## 8. Independent disposition of the suggested arrows

| Suggested arrow | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| preserved subject → proposed input | **Recovered only as an exact-Act-local role change; producing Act/occurrence and positive owner remain Unknown.** Preservation alone is insufficient. |
| candidate → proposed input | **Unknown.** Candidate production law does not establish proposal. |
| Demand → proposed input | **Unknown, with automatic production denied.** Demand does not produce even a candidate. |
| available Standing → proposed input | **Denied as automatic.** Availability/reachability creates possibility only; E.1 denies participation by availability and denies generic availability standing for the exact relation. |
| relation Standing → proposed input | **Unknown.** Warranted relations may receive Applicability, but warrant does not establish Applicability and no clause says it proposes the relation. |
| projection → proposed input | **Unknown.** Projection is neither named nor assigned as proposal production; visible/projected status cannot promote Standing. |
| caller reference → proposed input | **Unknown.** Reference/addressability does not establish native Standing, adoption, or the missing exact local assignment. |
| proposed input → Applicability determination | **Warranted duty.** The exact Act owner must ensure the determination before participation, consumption, or reliance. |

The first row does not assert that every preserved subject becomes proposed.
It classifies what the otherwise-unexplained crossing would have to change:
not the subject's existence or substantive Standing, but its local role relative
to an exact Act. Whether that role was actually assigned in any occurrence
still requires evidence not supplied by current active law.

## 9. Vocabulary audit result

The active-law uses resolve into distinct families rather than one pipeline:

- **proposed input** — E.1's local antecedent role for exact-Act Applicability;
- **proposal** — an act-shaped proposal is not occurrence, while translated or
  external material may carry attributed claim/relation/meaning proposals
  without Warrant;
- **candidate / candidate formation** — independently produced possible
  material with preserved formation dimensions, not proposal or Selection;
- **Demand** — separately established required-condition Standing, not a
  candidate or proposal producer;
- **input** — an Act-relative functional role; kinds and preservation
  requirements remain family-local;
- **participant** — a role in a relation, set, or Act, never implied by mere
  co-presence, proposal, or Applicable result;
- **Applicability** — an independently recoverable exact input-to-act
  determination owned by the exact Act Responsibility by default.

This audit supplies no evidence for a constitutional `Proposal` noun. The word
dissolves at this boundary into an already established exact-Act
Responsibility and its local input role, followed by that Responsibility's
Applicability duty. What remains unresolved is the positive occurrence that
assigns the role, not a warrant to construct a new universal responsibility.
