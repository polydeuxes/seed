## 3. Testimony-subject recovery

### Exact answer

`representation_relation=presented_alternative_represents_application_owned_source`.
Its `presentation_ref` and `scope` place that new assertion in the live presentation

Before that construction, no single material explicitly testifies to
`A represents G for P`:

* `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCES[A] == G` is the strongest evidence of application intent, but
  only a global lookup pairing. Calling it testimony merely because it maps two
  identifiers would erase the missing relation, attribution, purpose, and limits.
* `OPTIONS` declares `A` and its rendering but neither `G` nor `P`.
* `SOURCE_PROPOSITIONS` declares material about `G` but neither `A` nor `P`.
* Their combination supports a future testimony producer; combination alone is not
  a producer occurrence.
* The purpose declaration supplies exact `P`, but it does not supply the `A`–`G`
  relation and expressly denies formation.

Thus the exact testimony **does not currently exist before the attempted formation**.
The row is the first assertion and therefore cannot also serve, by its own
construction, as independently attributed input testimony proving its formation.
Mechanical construction proves only that an output can be built

## 4. Testimony ownership and attribution

The recoverable declaration responsibility is the **application representation
declaration**, not the source declaration, interaction, choice-set, or formation
occurrence:

* the application developer owns the coordinated declaration that the alternative
  identity is paired with this source identity;
* `OPTIONS` owns the alternative and its rendering;
* `SOURCE_PROPOSITIONS` owns source material;
* a future formation producer would consume testimony from those declarations but
  would not thereby become their declaration owner;
* formation responsibility owns examination and result, not the original assertion.

The exact current attribution is incomplete. The generated row says
`source_attribution = application-owned common-grammar prerequisite source` and its
provenance names `alternative-source-lineage:v1`, but neither identifies a testimony
supplier or producer declaration. The Event additionally calls the source
application-owned and labels responsibility `responsible-alternative-representation`
([runtime lines 1816–1835](seed_runtime/operator_ingress_common_grammar_prerequisite.py));
those post-construction strings do not establish declaration ownership.

The best bounded recovery is therefore:

```text
intended supplier: Seed application developer declaration (supported by nearby
application declarations and constant ownership)
exact testimony producer declaration: Unknown
constitutional declaration owner: application representation declaration
formation owner: a separate responsible formation producer
```

Module placement and constant ownership are provenance clues, not constitutional
ownership by identity. The intended supplier must be made explicit at a responsible
declaration boundary rather than inferred during formation.

## 5. Purpose, scope, provenance, and known-loss analysis

### Purpose binding

1. `ALTERNATIVE_SOURCES` is a global application mapping. It expresses no
   purpose-local relation.
2. `OPTIONS` contains no purpose material.
3. `SOURCE_PROPOSITIONS` contains no purpose material.
4. Exact `presentation_ref` is added to the choice set, and then to the row, only
   during mechanical construction. The row's `representation_purpose` remains the
   generic phrase “represent an application-owned bounded common-grammar source.”
5. Adding `presentation_ref` serializes a candidate purpose-local assertion; it does
   not responsibly bind generic application material to `P`. No current formation
   examiner consumes the exact `ApplicationPresentationPurposeDeclaration` or its
   `purpose_id`. Indeed the live caller constructs that declaration only for the
   eligibility call, discards the eligibility return, and later constructs the set
   and rows independently

The purpose declaration is nevertheless lawful input for future formation. It is
already the exact application-attributed declaration of `P`; a responsible examiner
need not invent another purpose. It must validate identity coherence between the
declaration, positive eligibility, and requested formation. Interpolation does not
perform that examination.

### Scope

The existing pairing's expressed scope is **the application generally for these
common-grammar constants**, because no narrower scope is declared. Its intended

The generated row narrows its serialized scope to one `presentation_ref` and one
choice-set reference. That is too late to establish the testimony's prior scope and
also combines two different claims. For formation, the warranted relation should be
bounded to **one exact presentation purpose `P`**. It need not be bounded to exact
choice set `C`; set membership belongs later.

### Known loss

The only declared loss says that the rendered label is compressed and does not carry
the complete source proposition (runtime lines 55–57). It is **rendering loss**. The
row carries the full proposition alongside the rendered fields, so current material

No current evidence establishes loss in the alternative-to-source identity relation;

| Loss coordinate | Standing |
|---|---|

| `A`-to-`G` identity relation | no known loss established |

### Conflicts and `Unknowns`

All current generated rows, purpose declarations, and relevant application
declarations initialize conflicts and `Unknowns` empty. Empty fields are testimony,
not proof that a responsible formation examiner checked them. Formation must detect
or preserve at least:

* conflict between testimony's `A`, `G`, or declared purpose/scope and requested
  formation;
* conflict between purpose declaration and the exact positive eligibility identity;
* missing or conflicting attribution, producer declaration, provenance, limits, or
  authority;
* ambiguity over the global mapping's intended scope;
* unsupported relation-level loss;
* any surviving upstream eligibility conflicts and `Unknowns` relevant to proceeding.

Missing required testimony or authority should yield `Unknown` or refusal according
to the responsible boundary's declared policy; the repository does not yet declare
that result policy.

## 10. Eligibility and participation boundaries

### Eligibility contribution

Positive eligibility contributes evidence that exact `G` may proceed toward
formation examination for exact `P`. A future formation boundary must consume the
**exact live eligibility Event as the occurrence-bearing input**, not merely
reconstruct a positive string. It should validate and preserve the narrower subset
relevant to its claim:

* result and exact eligibility assertion;
* source identity and exact purpose identity (`purpose_id` and `presentation_ref`);
* producer occurrence/lineage sufficient to identify the live return;
* applicable scope, provenance and authority limits;
* conflicts and `Unknowns`.

It need not copy the full recorded payload into its output. Consumption of the Event
as evidence and validation of these coordinates is enough; artifact duplication is
not constitutionally required.

Eligibility contributes **no** evidence that `A represents G`, no formation
authority, no positive formation result, and no exact-set participation. The
eligibility convention itself says it grants none (runtime lines 189–192).

### Participation separation

Formation testimony and authority should not require or assert `choice_set_ref`,
`exact_choice_set_fingerprint`, set membership, or presented token. Those coordinates
belong to the later participation/binding road. The exact purpose `P` is common
context, but it does not turn purpose-local formation into membership in `C`.

Formation may preserve rendered label/detail because the Book says responsible
formation preserves presented content and known loss where applicable. A token or
choice-set fingerprint becomes relevant only when a separate producer asks whether
the formed alternative participates in an exact set. Forming `A` for `P` remains
different from admitting `A` to `C`.
