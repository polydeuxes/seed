## 2. Error or overstatement being corrected

The prior report said that the operator-ingress specimen “lacks one concrete owner assignment” and treated the concrete-owner test as positive for a bounded gap. That overstates a general statement about a possible later acquisition. The specimen neither establishes an exact selected acquisition movement nor instantiates its authorization standing, recipient or responsible boundary, responsible act owner, or exact result. With no exact future instance yet established, the present **Unknown** is lawful non-instantiation, not an omitted assignment.

The overstatement also drove disposition B and a follow-up search for an acquisition authorization recipient and act owner. Searching merely to fill an uninstantiated coordinate would assume the future instance that the clause deliberately leaves possible. Both are retracted.

## 4. Corrected source analysis

### Lawful non-instantiation

`operator-ingress-common-grammar-prerequisite.md:139` says only that, after bounded operator goal establishment, *any later* acquisition movement and its authority require separate warrant and responsible occurrences. Line 143 likewise says a later responsible acquisition occurrence *may* make attributed resources or interpretation candidates available. Line 147 ends by denying that movement selection authorizes or causes movement occurrence. These clauses preserve a possible later boundary and its requirements; they do not establish that an exact later acquisition has been selected, authorized, or undertaken.

The distinction is therefore:

| Coordinate | Corrected finding |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| General future responsibility required | Yes. If a later acquisition is established, it requires separate warrant and a responsible occurrence. |
| Exact future owner presently identified | No; **Unknown**. |
| Exact future owner presently required to be identified | No, because no exact future act or authorization is presently instantiated. |
| Exact future act presently instantiated | No. The active text states a possibility and boundary conditions only. |
| Status of the present **Unknown** | Lawful non-instantiation, not omission. |

An authorization standing, once established, must preserve its granting source, recipient or responsible boundary, and exact authorized act or movement along with its other bounded coordinates (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`). That requirement does not compel the general operator-ingress clause to pre-name the owner of every possible future acquisition. Likewise, act grammar assigns responsibility at the exact act boundary: the responsible act consumes its subject, warrant, and conditions and produces or preserves its attributed result (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). It does not instantiate a future act from a stated possibility.

The granting source and authority boundary remain distinct. Operator expression or approval may contribute only after interpretation and scope binding (`authority-scope.md:10`), while a responsible authority boundary establishes authorization (`authority-scope.md:42`). Neither is collapsed into the later act owner.

### General grammar preserved

Selection does not establish authorization. Authorization standing requires a recipient or responsible boundary and the exact authorized act or movement. The responsible act consumes its subject, warrant, and conditions. Authorization standing does not establish act occurrence. No new universal role, deterministic rule, or additional relation family is required. These supported conclusions of the prior report are unchanged.

## 6. Corrected direct answers

1. **What does selection establish?** A bounded choice or lawful non-selection and its basis; it does not establish authorization or act occurrence.
2. **What must authorization standing preserve?** Its authority subject, granting source, recipient or responsible boundary, exact authorized act or movement, and the other bounded coordinates required by `selection-and-authorization.md:41`.
3. **Is an acquisition authorization already established in the specimen?** No. The clause states only what any later acquisition and its authority would require.
4. **Is an exact acquisition act already established?** No. No exact selected movement, authorization, subject, responsible occurrence, or result is instantiated.
5. **Has the Book omitted the owner of an already-formed act?** No. There is no already-formed exact act at this boundary.
6. **Why is the owner Unknown?** Because the possible future act and authorization do not yet exist, not because an assignment required for a present instance was omitted.
7. **Must a later authorization identify responsibility?** Yes. When established, it must name its recipient or responsible boundary and exact authorized act or movement; the responsible act must then consume its subject, warrant, and conditions.
8. **Does authorization standing establish occurrence?** No. Authorization and responsible act occurrence remain distinct.
9. **Are granting source and authority boundary identical?** Not by identity. Exact evidence would be required for one participant to occupy both coordinates in a particular instance.
10. **Is any new rule, role, or relation family needed?** No.
11. **Is amendment or an owner-search follow-up warranted?** No. Neither should be used to manufacture coordinates for a merely possible future instance.

## 7. Corrected final disposition

**A — existing authority and act grammar fully assign the responsibility; no concrete future act is instantiated in the specimen.**

Selection, authorization standing, and the responsible act already supply the general grammar at the final act boundary. The specimen lawfully leaves a future acquisition owner **Unknown** because it establishes no exact selected acquisition movement, exact authorization, subject, responsible act, or result. This is non-instantiation rather than omission. If a later exact authorization is established, it must still name its recipient or responsible boundary and exact authorized act or movement, and that standing does not itself establish act occurrence.

## 8. At most one follow-up

No follow-up. Do not construct, trace, or assign a future acquisition owner unless an exact authorized act is independently established.
