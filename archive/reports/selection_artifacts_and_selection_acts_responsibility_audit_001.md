## Direct answers

1. **Selection responsibilities.** The file independently establishes a bounded selection responsibility: consume a candidate set and basis, validate owner-required identity/applicability, choose zero or more candidates or preserve lawful non-selection, and produce an artifact that asserts and sufficiently preserves that result for its intended consumer. It separately establishes candidate-identity resolution as comparison, not selection.
2. **What is selected.** Candidates from an identified bounded candidate set are selected. In the stated representation illustration those candidates are registered read-model views/names. The file does not establish selecting an act or standing; selected-subject standing is an input a later consumer may adopt, while representation, candidate, and alternative wording is context-specific.
3. **Artifact versus act.** The act is the bounded occurrence that consumes, validates, and chooses/refuses. The artifact is the record asserting its result and preserving a consumer-sufficient basis, identity, uncertainty, or refusal. Constructibility and artifact presence do not prove the act.
4. **Selection-act inputs.** A bounded candidate set plus selection evidence, exact comparison, or policy, together with the identity and applicability required by the responsible owner.
5. **Possible result.** Zero or more chosen candidates, or a lawful preserved non-selection, bounded to that candidate set and basis.
6. **Artifact standing.** It has only bounded record/assertion standing for the selection result and its preserved basis. Its shape, construction, preservation, or acceptance as an artifact type does not establish occurrence, authorization, execution, downstream use, or reliance on every assertion.
7. **Owner of selection standing.** The responsible selector/owner boundary owns the selection occurrence and its result. The file does not name a universal selector service or assign selection standing to the artifact or consumer.
8. **Later consumer.** Only an intended consumer relative to the artifact's preservation contract is established generically. Such a consumer may locally adopt a selected-subject assertion, narrow a representation selection, or revalidate coherence; no universal downstream consumer is named.
9. **Downstream standing.** Selection establishes none of authorization, execution, inquiry opening, applicability, admission, use, or downstream standing by itself. Applicability may be validated as an owner-required input; that is not universal downstream applicability. Any explicit responsible boundary that adds a downstream result lies outside this audit.
10. **Negative-only distinctions.** All inequalities in the Important distinctions section, plus direct-construction and downstream non-authorization clauses, are negative authority where the file supplies no separate positive definition. In particular ranking, eligibility, uniqueness, authorization, execution, and artifact-type acceptance do not become positive selection grammar.
11. **Collapsed clauses.** No authoritative clause ultimately collapses identification, comparison, selection, authorization, or use: SAA-01 combines comparison/policy as a selection basis with the selection act but retains bounded inputs; SAA-06–07 separate identification; SAA-05 separates authorization/execution; SAA-08–09 preserve consumer-local use. The phrase “shared higher-order selection grammar” risks apparent collapse but is repository testimony followed by an express non-collapse rule.
12. **Unsupported kinds/results.** “Shared higher-order selection grammar,” “meaning selection,” “policy/work selection,” and “operational-realization selection” lack complete local responsibility and standing grammar. They remain testimony or unsupported hooks, not promoted kinds. “Selection record,” “ranked candidate,” “candidate eligibility,” and “authorization standing” receive only negative use here.
13. **External dependencies.** Constructor/production authority for artifacts, downstream authorization, road or consumer acceptance/reliance/use, composition, admission, and exact-key occurrence production are out of scope. They are not answered from adjacent files.
14. **Definitions/distinctions rather than responsibilities.** The constitutional subject and core question navigate; artifact/act and identity-resolution clauses define; the Important distinctions and counterexamples deny invalid inferences; repository anchors and named subfamilies illustrate implementation testimony. They are not separate Responsibility heads.
15. **Possible later Responsibility heads.** **Bounded selection and result preservation** is directly supportable. **Candidate-identity resolution** is separately supportable as a comparison responsibility, not a selection branch or proof. Representation/meaning/policy/work/operational-realization names, downstream uptake, coordinates, standings, participants, and denied comparisons cannot become heads from this file.

## Responsibility-head preparation

### Direct responsibilities, subordinate acts, relations, and results

- **Bounded selection and result preservation:** consume the exact candidate set and basis; validate owner-required identity/applicability; select zero or more or lawfully decline; assert the result; preserve intended-consumer-sufficient basis, identity, uncertainty, and refusal. The result is bounded selection standing or preserved non-selection, represented by but not identical to its artifact.
- **Candidate-identity resolution:** compare attributed testimony against a visible candidate set and establish, when warranted, one matching identity. Its result is identity resolution only.
- Selection relates a selector/owner boundary, candidate set, basis, selected result, artifact, and intended consumer. Consumer acceptance is a later local dependency and does not wholesale adopt producer assertions.
- Artifact construction authority, downstream authorization/admission/use, road standing, and composition consumption remain external dependencies.

### Rejected false heads

- Candidate set, evidence, comparison, policy, identity, applicability, basis, uncertainty, refusal, and purpose are inputs or coordinates, not heads.
- Candidate, alternative, selected name, selection artifact, selected result, lawful non-selection, and selected-subject standing are participants/results/standings, not heads.
- Selector and consumer are participant placeholders unless joined to their locally assigned acts.
- “Selection-to-consumer uptake” is a variable relation, not one responsibility established here.
- Authorization, execution, eligibility, ranking, uniqueness, exact deterministic comparison, and semantic judgment appear as distinctions or negative vocabulary, not additional selection heads.
- Named implementation subfamilies and runtime symbols do not create constitutional heads.

## Disposition

**F — unresolved external dependencies prevent disposition.** The bounded selection act and artifact boundary is locally clear, but assignment of artifact production authority and every downstream authorization, road, admission, reliance, and use consequence is deliberately external. The report preserves those dependencies instead of treating thematic adjacency as authority, so a complete downstream responsibility disposition cannot be made inside this file alone.

**Recommended follow-up:** None. External dependencies are explicitly recorded for independent recovery in their own governing boundaries.
