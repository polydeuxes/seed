# Artifact: substitution test 001

## 1. Executive

The question put: if `artifact` disappeared from Book proper, what exact
constitutional distinction would become impossible to express?

**None was found.** Every substantive clause survives substitution, and the
word's own definition is a union of two terms that already carry the content:

```text
artifact = preserved representation  OR  record
             carrying another responsibility's assertion
```

Three findings, in ascending order of weight.

**The positive uses are almost entirely structural.** Of 24 clauses that
survive a limiting-language filter, nine are markdown link text, three are
file titles, four are counterexample bullets, and the remainder are chapter
descriptions. The substantive clauses — `01.Kinds:10`, `:16`, `:19`, `:22` —
are all limiting, which is the pattern `#2346` recorded.

**No clause loses meaning under substitution.** §3.

**The chapter is titled for a standing its own definition denies.** §4. This
is the strongest evidence and it was reachable in one command.

This report proposes no amendment.

## 2. What `#2346` established, and what it left

`#2346` recovered:

```text
artifact names something                     yes
artifact is a carrier classification         yes
artifact owns a Responsibility               no
artifact is an Act                           no
artifact has independent Standing            no
artifact is a pipeline stage                 no
```

That verdict answered *what kind of thing it is*. It did not ask what would be
lost without it, and it declined to call the word a cat partly because
fourteen non-equivalences rely on having a word for the carrier role.

The stronger question is whether those non-equivalences need *this* word.

## 3. The substitution test

Each substantive clause, with `artifact` replaced by the exact thing it
describes.

**`01.Kinds:10`** — the definition. Substituting yields "a preserved
representation or record whose fields carry an assertion made by another
responsibility", which is the definition's own right-hand side. The sentence
is a *naming* sentence: it introduces a label for a union. Its content —
that such material carries another responsibility's assertion, that its shape
preserves identity, provenance, result, uncertainty, and boundaries, that it
supplies no warrant, and that standing comes from the production or
establishment boundary — attaches to "preserved representation or record"
without loss.

**`01.Kinds:16`** — "A produced, preserved, rendered, indexed, or otherwise
reachable **artifact** may become locally visible to a bounded consumer [...]
without becoming accepted, admitted, relied upon, projected as truth, written
to the event ledger, or authorized for mutation." The claim is that
reachability confers nothing. It holds for any reachable preserved material,
and the five adjectives already restrict the subject. Substitutable.

**`01.Kinds:19`** — "A kind label, **artifact form**, characterization
category, dataclass name, concordance entry, inventory row, or recurring
report type may preserve a bounded standing distinction." `artifact form` is
one of seven naming surfaces in a list. The other six already cover the
ground. Removable without loss.

**`01.Kinds:22`** — "Compiled behavior, **artifact construction**, stable
serialization, and compatibility evidence may witness what an observer,
projection, adapter, or other constructed mechanism does." `artifact
construction` reads as constructing a record or representation. Substitutable.

No clause required the word to state its distinction.

## 4. The chapter is named for a standing its definition denies

`artifact standing` as a phrase appears eight times in Book proper. Five are
markdown link text pointing at the chapter file. One is the chapter title. One
is the chapter's core question. One is a substring inside an unrelated clause.

**It appears in no clause as a term.**

The chapter is `Constitutional Kinds and Artifact Standing`, and its core
question is "what establishes an artifact's standing as one of them?" Its own
definition answers:

> Standing depends on the **kind-specific production or establishment
> boundary** and its validated inputs.

So the chapter title names a standing, the core question asks what establishes
it, and the definition replies that something else does. `#2346` reached the
same conclusion from a different direction — artifact has no independent
Standing — without noticing that the chapter structure asserts one.

That is historical compression surviving in the Book's own topology, which is
the shape `State` had before #2354: the noun living in titles and labels
rather than in claims.

## 5. What the word does that survives

Recorded because it is the strongest case for keeping it.

`artifact` is a **union**: preserved representation *or* record. Substituting
requires choosing one or writing both. Fourteen non-equivalences use the word
to name the carrier role — `act != artifact describing an act`,
`selection artifact != act of selection`, `Fact artifact != Fact standing`,
`artifact shape != constitutional kind`.

Those clauses do real work, and they would each need a subject. Whether
"preserved representation or record" is an acceptable subject in fourteen
places, or whether the union is worth a name, is a drafting judgement this
report does not make.

The prior finding stands: a shared word over subfamilies is grammar rather
than a kind, by the reading `02.Selection:16` applies to selection vocabulary.
The open question is whether Book proper should carry grammar that names no
subject, acts in no clause, and owns no standing.

## 6. What this does not establish

**That `artifact` should be removed.** The substitution test shows no
distinction is lost. It does not show the result reads well, and §5 records
the cost.

**That the fourteen non-equivalences survive without it.** Each was checked
for whether the *distinction* needs the word, not for whether a replacement
subject reads cleanly in that sentence.

**That the chapter should be renamed.** §4 records that the title asserts a
standing the definition denies. What the chapter should be called depends on
what it is about once `artifact` is settled, and that is not settled here.

**Anything about `record`.** The definition's other half was not tested. If
`artifact` is a union of `preserved representation` and `record`, then
`record` is load-bearing and has never been examined.

## 7. Method note

The decisive evidence took one command: counting where `artifact standing`
appears. Eight occurrences, and not one of them in a clause. A phrase that
exists only in a title, a core question, and five links to the file named
after it is a filename, not a term.

That check generalises. **A term used only in titles, filenames, and
cross-references is a navigation label**, and the campaign has now found the
same shape three times — `State` in chapter names, `Remembering` in
sentence-initial capitals, and `artifact standing` in link text. Each looked
like grammar because of where it appeared rather than what it claimed.
