## 2. Primary questions

### 2.1 What does the active Book currently establish as Responsibility?

Two distinct, non-identical things, and only these two:

1. **One of approximately eight co-equal macro-dimensional families.** `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:51-63` (01.Standing.F) lists "responsibility" as item 5 of eight: subject/identity, assertion/content, standing, source/provenance, **responsibility**, authority/warrant, scope/locality, occurrence/preservation. The clause is explicit that these are "broad constitutional questions, not mandatory fields or one universal artifact schema," that the paired labels are "bounded compressions of neighboring concerns, not declarations that every pair is one indivisible scalar," and that this orientation is "neither mathematically final nor closed." Nothing in this clause ranks responsibility above or below its seven siblings.
2. **Pervasive ordinary vocabulary for "who is accountable for this exact act."** Every chapter read for this recovery uses "responsible occurrence," "responsible boundary," "responsible act," "responsibility assigned to perform," or equivalent, dozens of times, as the working noun for the answerable party at a boundary. This use is real and load-bearing — it is how the Book expresses ownership everywhere — but it is ordinary compositional English applying a common noun, not a single named constitutional kind with its own artifact, schema, or dedicated chapter.

No active chapter defines "Responsibility" as a proper-noun constitutional kind with its own addressable boundary, the way `Demand`, `Gap`, `Fact`, `View`, `BOGE`, `Stopping`, or `Uptake` are each given a defining chapter or clause. There is no `Responsibility` counterpart to `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md`'s treatment of `artifact`, or to `demands-and-opened-movement.md`'s treatment of `Demand`.

### 2.2 Is Responsibility warranted as a macro-dimensional family, a structural HEAD, a constitutional subject, a parent of acts and results, some combination, or something narrower?

- **Macro-dimensional family: yes, already established** (01.Standing.F, item 5). This is settled, existing grammar, not a new recovery.
- **Structural HEAD for Book presentation: not currently established, but locally warranted.** See §2.10 and the topology artifacts in §4. No active clause makes Responsibility the parent of Authority, Standing, Evidence, Constraint, Consumer, or Stopping. `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:67` states directly: "Acts, constraints, and relations are structurally distinct from dimensions. They may operate over, govern, or connect dimension-bearing subjects under their independently owned constitutional grammar... Their presence in dimensional characterization does not make them additional dimensions, a mandatory intermediate layer, or one exhaustive taxonomy." This is a direct, active-Book denial that acts and constraints should be subordinated as children of a dimension (and Responsibility is itself one of the dimensions in that same clause). Promoting Responsibility to a universal parent of Act, Authority, Standing, Constraint, Consumer, and Stopping would contradict this clause as currently written, not merely extend it.
- **Constitutional subject: no.** "Constitutional subject" is itself an existing, distinct, already-instantiated structural element — the first heading of all 23 active chapters (e.g., `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:3-4`, `demands-and-opened-movement.md:3-5`). Responsibility is not that heading and does not currently occupy that structural role anywhere.
- **Parent of acts and results: partially, and only by implication, not by rule.** `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10` states "An act is a bounded responsibility at which something constitutionally occurs" — this is the single clearest textual point in the whole Book where "responsibility" is used as the *genus* for another constitutional kind ("act" is *a* responsibility, not the reverse). This is real evidence that "responsibility" is conceptually broader than "act" in at least this one clause, but it is one sentence in one chapter, not a general rule that every kind (Constraint, Standing, Consumer, Uptake, Stopping) is *also* a species of Responsibility.
- **Something narrower, and the strongest warranted answer:** Responsibility is well warranted as a **recurring analytical decomposition template** — a repeatable question set ("who is the responsible subject, what material, what act, under what authority and evidence, producing what result, consumed by whom, under what constraints, until what Stop") that nearly every chapter's Bounded resolution already implicitly answers, and that the two most recent independent audits in this repository (`examine_uptake_and_production_residue_audit_001.md` and `authorization_to_act_responsibility_audit_001.md`) both used explicitly and by name ("Responsibility decomposition" is section 5 of both) as their controlling method. This is demonstrated, working, repeatable practice — not a speculative proposal — but it is a **method for reading and auditing the Book**, not evidence that the Book's own kind-hierarchy should be restructured so that Responsibility becomes the literal parent node of Authority, Standing, Constraint, and the rest.

### 2.3 What distinction must remain between Responsibility, the responsible subject, the act, authority, standing, result, occurrence, consumer, relation, and constraint?

- **Responsibility != the responsible subject.** Responsibility is the accountable boundary/occurrence; the responsible subject is the exact material or subject it addresses (e.g., `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`: an act "consumes the subject plus the warrant and conditions").
- **Responsibility != the act.** `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10` makes act a species of responsibility ("An act is a bounded responsibility at which..."), not identical to it; a responsibility may exist without a completed act (e.g., a responsibility to determine applicability may find "inapplicable" and never reach an act, `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:41`).
- **Responsibility != authority.** Authority is a separately warranted standing requiring its own granting source, recipient/responsible boundary, scope, and evidence (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`; `authority-scope.md:42`). A responsibility may exist and be exercised without ever consuming authorization (e.g., a lens, a selection, an applicability determination), and authorization does not itself perform or constitute a responsibility's act (`authority-scope.md:44`, Authorization boundary correction 001).
- **Responsibility != standing.** Standing is the bounded posture a subject holds (applicable, admitted, established, current) as a *result* of a responsibility's exercise, not the responsibility itself (`constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:41`; `events-facts-and-state.md:10`).
- **Responsibility != result.** A responsibility "produces or preserves an attributed result" (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`); the result is downstream of the responsibility's exercise, and a failed or non-occurring act is still an exercise of the responsibility with a different result (absence-of-occurrence), not the absence of the responsibility (`constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:43`).
- **Responsibility != occurrence.** Occurrence must be evidenced by "the responsible validated production boundary" (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`) — the responsibility is the boundary that can produce occurrence evidence; occurrence is the evidenced fact that it did.
- **Responsibility != consumer.** A consumer independently owns Uptake and any consumer-local result and "need not be the grant source, authority producer, authorization recipient, or act owner" (`authorization_to_act_responsibility_audit_001.md` §5, grounded in `lenses-views-and-roads.md:12-20`). A responsibility's producer side and a later consumer's Uptake side are structurally distinct responsibilities, not one.
- **Responsibility != relation.** A relation is "its own bounded claim subject" with its own participants, roles, evidence, and producer (`constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:28`, 01.Standing.E); a relation may itself be the *result* of a responsibility's exercise (e.g., a meaning-relation warrant), but the relation and the responsibility that produced it are not the same thing.
- **Responsibility != constraint.** A constraint "consumes the proposed act or relevant context and produces or preserves a prohibition, requirement, condition, narrowing, or deferral applicable to that act" (`constraints-and-preconditions.md:10`); it governs or bounds a responsibility's act rather than being an instance of that responsibility, and `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:67` explicitly keeps constraints "structurally distinct from dimensions" and independently owned.

### 2.4 Which branches belong inside a Responsibility presentation?

Recovered as genuinely belonging (present, in some form, across the majority of chapters' Bounded resolutions): responsible subject/owner; material or subject addressed; the act; authority/warrant consumed; evidence/provenance; scope/locality/purpose; applicable standing produced or consumed; result; occurrence/preservation evidence. See the root topology in §4.1 for exact markings.

### 2.5 Which neighboring subjects must remain independently owned rather than becoming children?

Consumer/Uptake and Constraint are the two clearest cases: both are given their own independently-owned constitutional grammar elsewhere in the Book (`lenses-views-and-roads.md` for Uptake; `constraints-and-preconditions.md` for Constraint), and `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:67` explicitly denies that a dimension's presence in another kind's characterization makes it a subordinate part of that kind. Standing and Authority are similarly independently owned families with their own defining clauses (`selection-and-authorization.md`, `authority-scope.md`) that a Responsibility presentation may *reference* but must not *absorb*. Relation (01.Standing.E) is independently owned as its own bounded claim subject and must not be re-described as a Responsibility branch.

### 2.6 Which branches are always required to identify a responsibility?

Responsible subject/owner, material or subject addressed, and the act are required in every chapter examined — no chapter's Bounded resolution omits all three; omitting any one leaves the responsibility unidentifiable (this matches the defect pattern in §4.3, where omission of one of these three is exactly what several defects consist of).

### 2.7 Which branches are conditional on the exact responsibility and road?

Authority/warrant (many responsibilities, e.g. a lens or an applicability determination, consume no authorization at all); consumer/Uptake (only applicable once a result exists for someone else to take up); constraints (only applicable where a constraint boundary actually governs the act); Stopping (only applicable where the act's road can lawfully terminate before or instead of completion); occurrence/preservation (evidenced only where the responsible validated production boundary supplies it, per `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`); scope/locality/purpose (present wherever cited but its exact shape is subject-relative, per `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:65`).

### 2.8 What exact structural absence should remain uninstantiated rather than being called Unknown?

Per `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:69`, "inapplicable != Unknown." A branch is **not instantiated** (not Unknown) when the exact responsibility, road, or chapter never proposes that coordinate at all — e.g., a lens responsibility (`lenses-views-and-roads.md:10`) has no Stopping branch to be Unknown about; Stopping is simply not instantiated for a lens. A branch is **Unknown** only when an exact coordinate has been positively raised by a responsible occurrence and its value is unresolved — e.g., in the operator-ingress acquisition specimen, the recipient/responsible boundary for acquisition authorization *is* an exact required coordinate (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`) that the specimen simply never assigns a concrete value to (`operator-ingress-common-grammar-prerequisite.md:139`); that is a real Unknown, not a non-instantiated branch. This distinction is used throughout §3.

### 2.9 Where does the current Book leave acts, results, authority, consumers, or occurrences unowned or only recoverable through dense prose?

See the full defect map in §4.3. The clearest cases: the operator-ingress acquisition specimen's post-establishment owner chain (`operator-ingress-common-grammar-prerequisite.md:63-70,131,139,147`, already exhaustively mapped by `authorization_to_act_responsibility_audit_001.md`); the `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:69` "responsible examination or other kind-specific production occurrence" phrase (already exhaustively mapped by `examine_uptake_and_production_residue_audit_001.md`); and several chapters (notably `operator-ingress-common-grammar-prerequisite.md` itself, at 224 lines with 65 "Important distinctions" bullets) where the responsibility, act, and result for a given local road are recoverable only by reading multiple paragraphs of dense prose rather than from any structural marker.

### 2.10 What is the smallest active-Book amendment that can establish the root topology without reorganizing the whole Book at once?

A single new addressable boundary inside `constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md` (Book I already houses 01.Standing.F, the dimensional-characterization clause) that names the nine-branch responsibility-decomposition template as an **explicit, optional, recommended reading/audit lens** — not a required schema, not a restructuring of any existing chapter, and not a promotion of Responsibility above its seven sibling dimensions. See §4.4, Amendment 1.

## 4. Required topology artifacts

### 4.1 Responsibility root

```text
Responsibility
├── responsible subject / owner ............ required
├── material or subject addressed ........... required
├── exact responsible act .................... required
├── authority for that act ................... conditional (many responsibilities, e.g. lenses, applicability determinations, and selection acts, consume no authorization at all)
├── evidence and warrant ..................... required
├── scope, locality, and purpose ............. required (purpose is explicitly "a required local coordinate, not an additional universal dimension," constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:37)
├── applicable standing relations ............ conditional (produced or consumed only where the exact road instantiates one)
├── result .................................... required
├── occurrence and preservation .............. conditional (evidenced only by the responsible validated production boundary; a lens or applicability determination may never reach recorded occurrence)
├── consumer and Uptake ....................... independently owned neighbor — referenced by a responsibility's result, never subordinate to the producing responsibility (lenses-views-and-roads.md:12-20; constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:67)
└── constraints and lawful Stopping ........... independently owned neighbor — governs or may terminate the act, but is not a possession of the responsibility that performs the act (constraints-and-preconditions.md:39; constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:67)

not warranted as branches of Responsibility (each is its own root elsewhere in the Book, cited above):
  Authority (own chapter, authority-scope.md)
  Standing (cross-cutting, 01.Standing.F: "standing is recurrent and cross-cutting")
  Relation (own addressable boundary, 01.Standing.E)
  Constraint (own chapter, constraints-and-preconditions.md)
  Demand / Gap (own chapter, demands-and-opened-movement.md)
```

Six branches (subject, material, act, evidence/warrant, scope/purpose, result) are required in the sense that every responsibility-shaped chapter examined in §3 instantiates all six without exception; three (authority, standing, occurrence) are conditional in the sense that a real, positively-established responsibility may lawfully lack them; and two (consumer/Uptake, constraints/Stopping) are relations a responsibility's result may cross into, not branches the responsibility itself owns.

### 4.2 Responsibility instance examples

**Inquiry responsibility — frontier establishment** (`inquiry-frontiers.md:10`)
subject/owner: the consumer-local frontier-establishing boundary · material: warranted testimony about an inquiry boundary · act: establish bounded identity coherence and operative frontier standing · authority: not instantiated · evidence/warrant: the exact inquiry demand, source, provenance, scope, Unknowns, negative authority (04.Frontier.A) · scope/purpose: the present movement boundary or consumer-local context · standing: frontier standing/visibility, explicitly not selection or resolution · result: the bounded frontier · occurrence: not instantiated as a distinct preserved artifact requirement · consumer: a later responsibility taking up frontier material for its own declared act · constraints: not instantiated · Stopping: not instantiated

**Applicability responsibility** (`constitutional-kinds-and-artifact-standing.md:37-41`, 01.Standing.E.1)
subject/owner: "the responsibility assigned to perform an exact constitutional act," ordinarily the act owner, or an explicitly assigned occurrence · material: each proposed input · act: determine applicability for the exact input-to-act relation · authority: not required as a distinct coordinate here · evidence/warrant: consumer identity, source/provenance, standing/warrant, occurrence identity, known loss, conflicts, Unknowns, negative authority · scope/purpose: "purpose is a required local coordinate" · standing: applicable / inapplicable / conflicting / Unknown — and only these four · result: the applicability finding, nothing stronger · occurrence: not instantiated · consumer: the exact act that may or may not go on to consume the input · constraints: excluded inputs "may not participate in, be consumed by, or be relied upon" · Stopping: not instantiated — a lawful Stop "requires its own competent act" separately

**Authorization-related responsibility** (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`, `authority-scope.md:44`)
subject/owner: the responsible authority boundary · material: authority evidence, approval testimony · act: interpret, bind scope, establish authorization standing · authority: is itself the result, so the coordinate is reflexive — the boundary's own standing to establish authority must be independently evidenced · evidence/warrant: granting source, recipient/responsible boundary, act/movement authorized, temporal standing, constraints, occurrence, negative authority, Unknowns — eleven named coordinates, the most fully enumerated required-list in the Book · scope/purpose: named explicitly among the eleven · standing: bounded authorization standing · result: the authorization artifact/standing itself · occurrence: named explicitly among the eleven · consumer: the later act that consumes authorization as warrant · constraints: 08.Authority.A/B bound reliance and admission on the result · Stopping: not instantiated

**Producing/establishment responsibility — BOGE establishment** (`construction-and-establishment.md:12-16`)
subject/owner: the BOGE establishment occurrence, distinct from the eight upstream occurrences that feed it · material: the admitted warranted relation and its bounded proposition · act: rely on the admitted relation and consume the proposition · authority: consumer-local admission is the immediate gate; upstream authority for the meaning-relation warrant is a separate, earlier coordinate · evidence/warrant: preserved lineage, consumer-local evidence · scope/purpose: BOGE-local, explicitly not universal · standing: bounded operator goal standing · result: the established goal · occurrence: "the independently established external fact is only that the operator produced R after C was emitted" — a deliberately minimal occurrence claim · consumer: "ordinary bounded-goal advancement responsibilities," named only at role level · constraints: eight upstream responsibilities gate this one but are not its branches · Stopping: the sibling local-stop road is a structurally parallel, not nested, responsibility

**Stopping-related responsibility** (`stopping-and-completion.md`)
subject/owner: "a bounded responsible consumer" or "a responsible stopping occurrence at a competent boundary" · material: an evidenced non-movement condition · act: stop; preserve the exact supported reason · authority: not a required coordinate by name, though "operator boundary" and "authority gaps" are listed among the conditions a Stop may respond to · evidence/warrant: the exact distinguishable reason (supported irrelevance, unsupported material, missing required binding, outside-authority standing, or an Unknown from unresolved binding) · scope/purpose: the exact bounded goal or act being stopped · standing: a bounded Stop, or the stronger, separately-warranted completion claim · result: the Stop itself, carrying its preserved reason · occurrence: the stopping occurrence · consumer: not this chapter's concern; a later reader of the preserved reason · constraints: "does not make Stopping the owner of the reason-producing responsibility... that a Stop may later consume as its reason" — an explicit anti-absorption clause · Stopping: reflexive; this chapter is Stopping's own home

They are not normalized into one shape here, per the prompt's instruction.

## 5. Structural test direction

The existing Markdown heading hierarchy already gives every chapter a `## Constitutional subject`, `## Core question`, `## Bounded resolution`, and (where applicable) numbered addressable-boundary headings (e.g. `### 01.Standing.F`), `## Important distinctions`, `## Representative repository anchors`, `## Counterexamples or failure modes`, and `## Related chapters`. A structure-level test does not need to parse or validate prose meaning to check:

- **Responsibility HEAD present** — not testable today, because no chapter currently declares a `Responsibility` heading; testable only after (or as part of) Amendment 1, and only as "does the named addressable boundary exist," not as a schema-validity check.
- **Required direct branches present** — testable per-chapter only in the weak sense of "does the Bounded resolution text contain recognizable act/result/subject language"; this recovery found no chapter missing responsible subject, material, act, or result, but confirming that mechanically would require prose parsing, not structure parsing, and is not recommended as a first structural test.
- **Branch ownership explicit** — testable as "does each named responsible occurrence in a chapter appear as its own heading or clearly delimited paragraph," which is exactly the gap Amendments 4 and 5 propose to close; before that, this is not mechanically testable without prose parsing.
- **Independently owned neighbors not nested falsely** — testable today as a heading-hierarchy check: Constraint, Uptake/Consumer, Authority, and Stopping already have their own top-level chapter files (`constraints-and-preconditions.md`, `lenses-views-and-roads.md`, `authority-scope.md`, `stopping-and-completion.md`); a test could assert that no future chapter nests one of these as a subheading under a different chapter's addressable-boundary numbering scheme (e.g., no `01.Responsibility.C` heading whose text is actually Constraint's or Authority's subject matter). This is the most immediately testable item in this list, and requires no Book amendment to check for going forward.
- **No duplicate branch identities** — testable today as a cross-chapter heading-text or addressable-boundary-ID uniqueness check (no two chapters claiming the same `NN.Family.X` identifier); this is a pure structure check already possible against the current file set.
- **No Unknown branch without an exact coordinate** — not mechanically testable from heading structure alone; this requires the same distinction this report applied by hand in §2.8 and §3 (Unknown requires a positively-raised exact coordinate, not just the literal word "Unknown" appearing near a heading), and would need a prose-aware check, not a structure-only one.

No YAML, JSON, registry, class hierarchy, or manifest is proposed. No test is implemented in this report.
