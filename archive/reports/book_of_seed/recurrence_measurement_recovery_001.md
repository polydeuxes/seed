# Warranted recurrence: recovery 001

## 1. Executive

The question: if `learn` appears in E2 and again in E3, can Seed warrant that
the same representation recurred — without knowing what it means?

**Yes, and active law already says so, in a clause written for exactly this.**
No new act, noun, or grammar is required. `01.External.E` is titled:

> **Measurement and recurrence do not establish meaning**

and `01.External:28` grants the finding and states its conditions in one
sentence:

> A **declared measurement** may produce bounded findings of exact equality,
> count, **recurrence**, prefix occurrence, the result of a declared predicate,
> or adjacency within its measurement boundary. Those findings do not establish
> structural, grammatical, or semantic meaning, or constitutional standing
> beyond the measurement assertion. **A recurrence assertion must disclose the
> representation or projection measured, the rule by which equivalence or
> sameness was determined, and the bounded scope within which occurrences were
> counted.** A consumer's purpose separately governs lawful reliance,
> acceptable aggregation, and acceptable representational loss; purpose is not
> therefore a required coordinate of every exact count.

Three findings follow.

**The act is declared measurement, not Compare.** This was expected to be
Compare. It is not. `measurement` has 65 occurrences in active law and is
distinguished from neighbouring acts (`operational measurement != recording`).
Recurrence is a *finding type* a declared measurement may produce, alongside
exact equality, count, prefix occurrence, declared-predicate result, and
adjacency.

**The tokenizer question is already answered as a disclosure requirement.** The
worry — that introducing a tokenizer smuggles in a semantic decision — maps
exactly onto the clause's second required disclosure: *the rule by which
equivalence or sameness was determined*. A tokenizer is not forbidden. It must
be **declared**, and the assertion must carry it.

**Counts are contemplated.** "Acceptable aggregation" and "every exact count"
are in the clause. `learn occurs 417 times in E3` is the same finding type as
`learn recurs across E2 and E3`.

## 2. What Seed may and may not warrant

The clause's limit is in its title, and three separate non-equivalences guard
it across three chapters:

```text
01.External:28   findings "do not establish structural, grammatical, or
                 semantic meaning, or constitutional standing beyond the
                 measurement assertion"
01.External:41   lexical recurrence != constitutional authority
06.Ownership:15  naming recurrence != shared responsibility
```

So for the operator's example:

```text
WARRANTABLE as declared-measurement findings
  the representation "learn" occurs in E2
  the representation "learn" occurs in E3
  "learn" recurs across E2 and E3
  "learn" occurs N times within E3
  the sequence "learn english" recurs

NOT ESTABLISHED by any of them
  the two occurrences of "learn" mean the same thing
  E3 concerns E2's request
  E3 teaches English
  meaning("learn")
  any relation between E2 and E3 beyond the recurrence itself
```

The gap between those two lists is the whole value of the clause. A recurrence
finding is real Standing and it is narrow Standing, and the narrowness is
what makes it available without language.

## 3. Why this is a different rung from occurrence order

`#2350` recorded witnessed append order as available from the observing
boundary's own Authority — direct warrant. Recurrence is not that. It requires
a declared measurement over two independently preserved representations, and
the measurement has disclosure obligations that mere witnessing does not.

```text
occurrence order   direct warrant; the boundary witnessed the sequence
recurrence         a declared measurement act, with three required
                   disclosures, over preserved representations
```

Both stay entirely inside what Seed can observe about its own material.
Neither requires meaning. But recurrence is an act Seed *performs*, not a fact
it *witnesses*, and the clause holds it to a correspondingly higher standard of
disclosure.

`03.Prerequisite:57` shows the finding type already in use elsewhere in active
law — "repeated obstruction may produce **recurrence evidence**." Recurrence
produces evidence, which a later responsible act may consume as evidence
without inheriting strength (`05.Testimony:24`).

## 4. Do not mint `Token`

Recorded before it happens, since the campaign's whole record predicts it.

`token` is not established Book vocabulary for this. The clause's own words for
the measured thing are **representation** and **projection** — both heavily
established — and its word for the sameness rule is just that: *the rule by
which equivalence or sameness was determined*.

```text
proposed          established
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Token             representation, or projection
tokenizer         the declared rule by which equivalence or
                  sameness was determined
TokenMatch        a recurrence finding of a declared measurement
```

A tokenizer is an implementation of the sameness rule. It is a coordinate the
assertion must disclose, not a constitutional kind.

## 5. A correction to #2345

`#2345` cited `01.External:28` as the single appearance of `equivalence` in
active law and characterised it as "a limiting clause." That reading was
one-sided. The clause is a **grant with limits**: it authorises a class of
findings and then bounds what they establish. `#2345`'s conclusion — that
`equivalence` does not name a constitutional kind — is unaffected, since the
clause uses the word to describe a sameness rule rather than to name a subject.
But describing the clause as merely limiting missed that it is the positive
authority for the entire recurrence capability recovered here.

Recorded because the pattern is worth naming: reading a clause for what it
denies and stopping there. `01.External:28` was read twice in this campaign
before its grant half was noticed.

## 6. What this does not establish

That any recurrence measurement is currently implemented. Nothing was checked
in the runtime. `01.External:28` establishes what such an act must disclose,
not that Seed performs one.

That a recurrence finding is available on the ingress material specifically.
The clause requires a declared measurement boundary and a disclosed
representation-or-projection; whether the preserved ingress representations
satisfy that is unexamined.

That recurrence is the right next rung. It is *a* lawful rung, cleanly
authorised, requiring no meaning. Whether to take it is a separate judgement,
and `01.Uptake.A` denies any universal order among such acts anyway.

That accumulated recurrence findings warrant anything further. `05.Testimony:111`
holds that history establishes no trajectory without a separate comparison
boundary, and `01.External:28` bounds each finding to its own measurement
assertion. A ladder of narrow findings does not become a broad one by getting
longer.

## 7. Method note

The answer was found by grepping the word the operator used. `recurrence`
returns a clause heading — `01.External.E` — written for precisely this
question, including the disclosure list that the tokenizer worry had
independently reconstructed.

That is the fourth time in this campaign that a question the participants
worked out from first principles turned out to be already answered in active
law: the meaning-assertion pair at `01.Kinds:32`, the warranting occurrence at
`01.Kinds:32`/`05.Provenance:42`, the ledger-order limit at `06.Events:47`, and
now this. The pattern suggests searching the Book with the *operator's own
word* before reconstructing, because the reconstruction tends to arrive at the
clause anyway, with less precision and a new noun attached.
