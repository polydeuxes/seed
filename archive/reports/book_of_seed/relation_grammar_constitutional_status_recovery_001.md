# Relation grammar: constitutional status recovery 001

## 1. Executive

`relation grammar` does not name a constitutional subject. It is the ordinary
`<X> grammar` construction, which throughout active law means *the established
grammar concerning X* — a body of rules and vocabulary through which
responsibilities are expressed, never a subject that is produced, owned, or
given standing.

The phrase `applicable relation grammar` therefore has no Producer, no owning
Responsibility, no formation occurrence, no Standing, and no coordinates,
and requires none. It maps onto an already-established coordinate:

```text
applicable relation grammar
  → each applicable constitutional dimension of the exact relation
    (01.Kinds:28)
```

Five findings support this, in ascending order of weight.

**The phrase is absent from active law.** Zero occurrences of `relation
grammar` or `relation-grammar` in any active-law chapter. Every occurrence
sits in a report: 45 uses of the exact phrase `applicable relation grammar`
across eight reports, and 83 uses of `relation grammar` across thirteen
(counts exclude this report).

**It was introduced during a correction, not recovered.** The file that now
carries it earliest, `grammar_bounded_orientation_observation_001.md`, was
created at #2240 with **zero** uses. #2244 — a correction round — introduced
the compound noun on eleven lines at once. It never had a recovery.

**Active law uses the construction twenty ways and gives none of them an
owner.** The complete set: artifact, bounded, Claim, closed-choice, common,
concern, constitutional, durable, external, family, implementation, movement,
observation, prose, representation, Seed-native, selection, shared, source,
and stopping grammar. Searching active law for a `Grammar` Responsibility,
Act, Standing, Producer, or owner returns nothing.

**The closest parallel is explicitly denied act-kind status.** `selection
grammar` is the sharpest test available, because Selection *is* an established
act with recovered owners. `02.Selection:16`
(`02-acts-and-constraints/selection-artifacts-and-selection-acts.md`) says:
"Repository evidence supports a shared higher-order selection grammar with distinct subfamilies... Shared
selection vocabulary does not collapse these into one constitutional act
kind." The grammar names the shared family; the act kinds sit beneath it and
hold the coordinates. `relation grammar` is the same construction, and by the
same reading the *relation* holds the coordinates.

**Two clauses state positively what grammar is.** `03.Prerequisite:148`: internal
responsibilities "are expressed **through** prerequisite, Demand, movement,
selection, authority, and stopping grammar" — six instances of the
construction in one list, all naming a medium of expression. And
`01.External:10`, which is the decisive one because it addresses standing
directly: "A distinction expressed through external grammar may be
constitutionally recovered and warranted; **the recovered distinction receives
standing, while the source grammar remains attributed external grammar**."
What receives standing is the distinction. The grammar does not.

## 2. The prior question

The campaign was preparing to ask what produces the relation grammar, who owns
it, what Evidence forms it, and how a candidate is compared against it. Those
questions all presuppose that the phrase names something.

The check established at #2338 runs first:

```text
Before recovering what owns X, establish that X names something.
```

This is the third consecutive application. `closed-choice` survived as a
Presentation subtype until the same test dissolved it; `proposed input`
survived as a boundary coordinate until #2338 dissolved it; `relation grammar`
survives here only as far as the construction it belongs to.

The cat test, stated for this case:

```text
The cat jumped the fence.
"jumped fence" does not establish JumpedFence Standing.

Likewise:
"applicable relation grammar" does not establish RelationGrammar
merely because the phrase parses as a noun phrase.
```

## 3. What the phrase is reaching for

Applying the mapping discipline — for any new word, ask which already-
established word it reaches for, before the test battery.

`01.Kinds:28` gives the relation its own dimensions:

```text
A relation is its own bounded claim subject. A relation claim or standing
must preserve each applicable constitutional dimension, or its explicit
Unknown or unresolved standing, including participants and roles, the
relation assertion, evidence standing, scope, producer, consumer and
purpose, authority, occurrence, conflicts, and limits.
```

Ten named dimensions, already established, already owned. The word shared
between the compound noun and the clause is **applicable** — `applicable
relation grammar` against `each applicable constitutional dimension`. The
compound noun is a compression of the clause that already exists.

Nothing is lost by the longer form. Taking the heaviest use verbatim:

```text
before  Compare the candidate equivalence with the applicable
        relation grammar.

after   Compare the candidate equivalence against the constitutional
        dimensions applicable to the relation it proposes.
```

The second is longer and says the same thing, with the advantage that its
coordinates are locatable.

## 4. What was built on it

The compound noun did not stay inert. Two coordinate assignments were made to
it in `candidate_versus_grammar_comparison_recovery_001.md`:

```text
:161  "The candidate equivalence and the applicable relation grammar
       are the exact compared subjects."
:166  "Candidate Provenance, relation grammar Evidence, and Warranting
       material as applicable."
```

A compared subject and its own Evidence. Under this recovery neither holds:
there is no second subject, and a body of governing dimensions does not carry
Evidence of its own.

The reification is visible at the moment of introduction. #2244 wrote both
forms into a single paragraph:

```text
"The applicable relation grammar examines that candidate."
"Different relation-shaped candidates must be examined through the
 grammar appropriate to the relation they propose."
```

The second sentence is the ordinary reading. The first makes the grammar the
**agent** of examination. A standard does not examine anything; a responsible
act examines a candidate *under* an applicable standard. The compound noun
acquired agency in the same paragraph that stated the plain version.

## 5. Consequence to check, not settled here

Shape B is rendered throughout as *compare the candidate equivalence with the
applicable relation grammar*. That rendering is a two-subject comparison, and
`05.Testimony.E:27` scopes bounded comparison to "multiple independently
preserved **testimonies or findings**."

Governing dimensions are neither a testimony nor a finding. So Shape B as
rendered does not fit the clause it was placed under. What it describes looks
instead like evaluation of one subject against a standard:

```text
compare two preserved testimonies      05.Testimony.E, established
evaluate one candidate against the     not the same act; owner and
  dimensions governing its relation      warrant not recovered here
```

This report does **not** conclude that Shape B dissolves, that no Compare
occurs at that boundary, or that the second act is unlawful. It records that
Shape B's exact rendering names a two-subject comparison whose second subject
does not exist, and that the act actually described has not been recovered
under that description. That recovery is the next question, and it is now a
question about the *comparing act*, not about where a grammar object comes
from.

## 6. What this does not establish

The absence of `relation grammar` from active law is decisive against the
claim that it was **recovered**, because the reports asserted recovery. It is
not an argument against **constructing** something in that position later. The
Book is a single witness and its silence is not a fact about the world.

Nor does this touch: relations as bounded claim subjects (`01.Kinds:28`,
untouched and load-bearing here); the relation dimensions themselves; PESC as
a bounding discipline; grammar-bounded orientation as an observation; or the
Applicability recoveries in the affected reports, which never depended on the
compound noun being a subject.

## 8. Correction record

**Defect: a compound noun entered through a correction round and was
subsequently treated as recovered.** #2244's subject was correcting PESC
equivalence. The compound noun arrived as drafting inside that correction, and
every later report cited it as established vocabulary. No report asked where it
came from.

Corrections are trusted more than the drafts they fix, because a correction is
read as the result of scrutiny. That makes new vocabulary introduced *during* a
correction the least examined material in the corpus.

**How to apply:** when a term is load-bearing, locate the commit that
introduced it and read what that commit was for. A term introduced by a
commit whose stated subject was something else has not been recovered — it has
been drafted, and the burden is still open.
