## 5. Responsibility decomposition

**Granting source.** Operator expression or approval is testimony until interpretation and scope binding (`authority-scope.md:10`). The operator can be the granting source preserved in authorization standing, but the clause does not make the operator the Seed-side authority-establishing boundary (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`; `authority-scope.md:42`).

**Authority-establishing boundary.** Only a responsible authority boundary establishes bounded authorization (`authority-scope.md:42`). It must preserve both the granting source and “recipient or responsible boundary,” proving those are separate coordinates rather than identities. The clause does not say that authority establishment is the later act.

**Recipient or responsible boundary.** General law requires authorization standing to name it (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`). This is already the role-coordinate for the later responsible owner; no new universal role is missing. However, the operator-ingress acquisition specimen says only that later movement and authority need separate responsible occurrences (`operator-ingress...:139`) and never instantiates the recipient/responsible boundary for the exact movement.

**Owner of later act.** Act law requires an independent responsible occurrence consuming subject, warrant, and conditions and producing/preserving a result (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). Authorization standing is part of the warrant/conditions for an authorized act, but it neither causes nor proves occurrence (`selection-and-authorization.md:39-41`; `authority-scope.md:42`).

**Consumer of result.** A later consumer independently owns Uptake and any consumer-local result (`lenses-views-and-roads.md:12-20`). It need not be the grant source, authority producer, authorization recipient, or act owner.

## 6. Existing grammar matches

The **missing-rule test** is negative. General selection law supplies selection (`selection-artifacts-and-selection-acts.md:10`); authority law supplies bounded authorization and requires its recipient/responsible boundary (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`; `authority-scope.md:42`); act law supplies the responsible occurrence shape (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). Adding a rule by which accumulated standing causes an act would contradict the explicit identity separations in `selection-and-authorization.md:39-41`.

The **missing-role test** is also negative. “Recipient or responsible boundary” is already a required authorization coordinate (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`). An authorized producer is also required to validate warrant before asserting occurrence (`constructors-and-production-authority.md:10`). No new named role is constitutionally justified.

The **concrete-owner test** is positive for a bounded gap. The proving specimen assigns BOGE, comparison, identification, recovery, admission, and establishment occurrences with varying specificity, but after goal establishment it says only “ordinary bounded-goal advancement responsibilities” and requires later responsible occurrences (`operator-ingress...:63-70,131,139`). Its final sentence denies that movement selection authorizes or performs movement (`:147`). It does not identify the exact recipient/responsible boundary in an acquisition authorization or identify the owner of the later acquisition act. General grammar is complete; the concrete assignment is **Unknown**.

The **producer-to-consumer-road test** does not justify a separate missing constitutional road. Once an exact authorization instantiates its required recipient/responsible boundary and exact act/movement, the act owner consumes its warrant under act grammar (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`; `selection-and-authorization.md:41`). What is absent in the specimen is the instantiation itself, not a new relation family. Implementation realization was not inspected and no claim about its presence is made.

## 7. Missing-boundary analysis

The visitor correctly identified the negative endpoint: the proving clause says “movement selection neither authorizes nor performs movement” (`operator-ingress...:147`). The inference that a new constitutional responsibility follows is not established. Existing authority grammar already requires the authorization to name the recipient/responsible boundary and exact act/movement (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`), and act grammar already assigns result-producing responsibility to the later occurrence (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`).

What remains is narrower: for this proving specimen, no active clause identifies which concrete boundary receives bounded acquisition authorization and owns the exact acquisition occurrence. `operator-ingress...:139` demands separate warrant and responsible occurrences but does not instantiate either owner. This is a concrete owner assignment left **Unknown**, not a missing deterministic rule, new role, or independently proven road.

The “hereby” questions therefore resolve as follows. The exact responsible occurrence would be the authorized acquisition act, but its concrete owner is **Unknown** in the specimen (`operator-ingress...:139`). Its independent warrant must include bounded authorization plus the subject and conditions demanded by act law (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). The subject is the exact acquisition movement/material selected and authorized, but no exact candidate is supplied by this general specimen. The result would be the attributed result of that acquisition—`operator-ingress...:143` allows a later responsible acquisition occurrence to make attributed external-grammar resources or interpretation candidates available—but its exact shape remains **Unknown**. Occurrence needs evidence from the responsible validated production boundary; an artifact or accumulated evidence is not occurrence (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`; `constructors-and-production-authority.md:10`).

BOGE is a valid proving specimen only for the local distinctions it states. The Book warns that its topology is not compulsory or universal (`operator-ingress...:13,85`) and that the closed-choice case is one ordinary bounded use (`:95-97`). It is not a reusable universal approval apparatus.

## 9. Direct answers

1. **What does selection establish?** A bounded choice or lawful non-selection among candidates and its basis (`selection-artifacts-and-selection-acts.md:10`; `selection-and-authorization.md:39`).
2. **What does authorization standing establish?** A separately warranted bounded authority standing preserving the authority subject, granting source, recipient/responsible boundary, exact act/movement, scope, purpose, temporal standing, constraints, evidence, occurrence, negative authority, and Unknowns (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`).
3. **Does it name its recipient or responsible boundary?** It is required to do so (`selection-and-authorization.md:41`); the proving specimen does not instantiate that coordinate.
4. **Does act grammar require an independent responsible occurrence?** Yes. An act consumes subject, warrant, and conditions and produces/preserves an attributed result (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`).
5. **Does any active clause assign the exact owner for the specimen?** No. `operator-ingress...:139` requires later responsible occurrences but does not identify the acquisition authorization recipient or acquisition act owner.
6. **Is granting source the authority-establishing boundary?** Not by identity. They are separate coordinates (`authority-scope.md:10,42`). Whether one participant occupies both in a particular authorization is **Unknown** absent exact evidence.
7. **Is authority-establishing boundary the act owner?** Not by identity; authorization establishment and act occurrence have distinct clauses (`authority-scope.md:42`; `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). Coincidence in an exact case requires evidence.
8. **Is the operator only granting source, or more?** In this specimen the operator is positively the source of response material (`operator-ingress...:25-27,105`) and may be the granting source if later approval testimony warrants it (`authority-scope.md:10`). Operator selection, authorization, authority-boundary ownership, and later act ownership remain **Unknown** (`operator-ingress...:105`).
9. **What is lacking?** Not a general rule or role and not a newly required road. The specimen lacks one concrete owner assignment; implementation realization is outside this audit.
10. **Does accumulated evidence cause an act by identity?** No. Selection, approval testimony, request, invocation, and records do not grant authorization or prove occurrence by identity (`selection-and-authorization.md:39-41`; `authority-scope.md:42`; `acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`).
11. **What is consumed by the later act?** The exact subject plus warrant and conditions (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`), including the bounded authorization standing for an authorized movement. The specimen's exact subject/authorization artifact is **Unknown**.
12. **What result is produced?** Act grammar requires an attributed result (`acts-and-act-artifacts.md:10`). For the acquisition example, newly available attributed resources or interpretation candidates are possible (`operator-ingress...:143`), but the exact result remains **Unknown** until the exact act is assigned.
13. **BOGE status?** A valid local proving specimen, not a reusable universal precedent (`operator-ingress...:13,85,95-97`).
14. **One-time approval versus bounded reusable authorization?** Distinct. Approval is testimony; selected-for-consideration is not reusable authorization; authorization has explicit temporal/scope coordinates (`selection-and-authorization.md:10,41`). Reusability in any exact grant remains bounded and must be established, not inferred.
15. **Warranted amendment or follow-up?** No amendment is established by this report. One bounded follow-up may test whether the acquisition specimen can positively identify its authorization recipient/responsible boundary and exact act owner from active authority not already examined; preserve **Unknown** otherwise. No implementation recommendation is made.
